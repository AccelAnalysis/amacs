# AMACS Commercial Capability Classification

## Operating architecture versus market capability

AMACS distinguishes between two different questions:

1. **What machinery does the standard use?**
   Governance rules, concept registries, evidence records, response sections, decision factors, release controls, schemas, and APIs are operating components of AMACS.
2. **What can an organization provide to the market?**
   Matchable capabilities are selectable taxonomy concepts that an organization may assert, an issuer may require, and the RFxchange may use for discovery, teaming, gap analysis, and response evaluation.

AMACS `0.1.0` answered the first question for standards governance, evidence, response, and decision architecture, but did not answer the second question explicitly. Release `0.2.0` closes that partial classification gap.

## Commercial use

These records make the services discoverable without implying that every organization claiming them is qualified. An organization-capability assertion must still preserve the AMACS release, label snapshot, delivery role, service geography, evidence status, and applicable qualifiers.

The capabilities may be used independently or in combination. For example:

- taxonomy development may be paired with standards governance and capability mapping;
- evidence architecture may be paired with response and decision architecture; and
- controlled taxonomy licensing may be paired with taxonomy API delivery.

## Licensing boundary
